* **MARS: Multi-hop Adaptive Retrieval and SPARQL Generation for KGQA** — a new Text-to-SPARQL model.
  * Paper: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14561>; code: <https://github.com/dice-group/mars-kgqa>
  * Fixed procedure, adaptive depth: entity linking → retrieve and rank graph patterns → LLM reasoning → expand another hop or generate SPARQL.
  * Entity linking is based on GRASP.
  * It retrieves **patterns, not raw triples**, to keep the context small.
  * No dataset-specific fine-tuning; evaluated on QALD-9-plus, QALD-10 and LC-QuAD 2.0.
  * The paper reports that MARS **outperforms GRASP in 8 of 14 head-to-head comparisons**, especially on QALD-10 and LC-QuAD 2.0.
  * **My view:** MARS looks strong for Text-to-SPARQL translation, but less suited to probing KG incompleteness. Its multi-hop exploration is constrained by what is already present in the KG around the linked entities, whereas zero-shot exploration can hypothesize missing structure by analogy with more complete subgraphs.

## October 1 — Main Conference Day 2

### Keynote: Paul Groth — *The Knowledge Placement Problem*

* Paul explicitly framed the keynote as **raising questions rather than answering them**.
* Core question: where should knowledge live — model weights, context, retrieval index, agent memory, KG / database, tools, or people?
* LLMs make it easier to move knowledge between representations, but every transformation can be costly and lossy.
* **Requirements shape placement**: mutability, verifiability, attribution, composition, capacity, read / write behaviour, workload and time all matter.
* **Provenance is a key building block**: trace sources, transformations, placements and downstream uses so that corrections can propagate.
* KGs may be the **connective tissue** across heterogeneous stores rather than the only place where knowledge lives.
* Sequence most relevant to us: multimodal knowledge → multimodal tables → multiple useful representations → **representations are inconsistent (KONTRAST)** → knowledge changes over time (**EMERGE**).
* Conclusion: foundation models do not reduce the scope of Knowledge Engineering; they make the playground much bigger.

### Random notes from Paul's keynote

* **EMERGE: A Benchmark for Updating Knowledge Graphs with Emerging Textual Knowledge** — studies dynamic knowledge management and how to update a KG.
  * Paper: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.03617>; code: <https://github.com/klimzaporojets/emerge>
  * Accepted at NeurIPS 2026 Datasets & Benchmarks.
  * The problem is not only extracting facts from text, but deciding how new textual evidence should **change the current KG state**.
  * Operations include **Exists, Add, Mint+Add, Infer, Deprecate**.
  * Natural continuation after KONTRAST: detect disagreement → understand temporal change → decide whether / how the KG should be updated.

* [PixelRAG](https://pixelrag.ai/) — search Wikipedia by what it looks like; visual search over Wikipedia.
* [Never-Ending Learning for Agentic AI Systems tutorial](https://megagon.ai/agentic_tutorial2026/) — useful for dynamic knowledge, drift, memory and continuously changing systems.

### Session 7: Socio-technical Aspects of Knowledge Engineering

* Recurring theme: **the role of humans in knowledge modelling** — who defines entities, identifiers, exceptions and boundaries, and what gets lost when these choices are formalized?
* Zeno van Cauter et al. — qualitative study of entities / identifiers / identification in industrial manufacturing.
* Alonso Ruiz-Palomo and Carlos Badenes-Olmedo — QUILL: evaluates whether LLM-generated knowledge cues actually help human deductive reasoning.
* Gabriele Sacco et al. — *Formal and Human Reasoning with Exceptions*.
* Anna Wróbel, Elia Meregalli and Stefan Schlobach — **How to Unravell Implicit Power Relations in Symbolic AI Research** — **Best Research Paper Award**.
  * Build a **Research-Power Ontology (RPO)** and **Research-Power KG (RP-KG)** to study normally implicit power relations in Semantic Web / AI research.
  * Dataset: **1,072 ISWC + ESWC papers (2015–2024)**; enriched with OpenAlex, Crossref, CS-KG, NLP and Wikidata; resulting KG has **600k+ triples**.
  * Interesting competency questions: *Who funds the methods / materials? Are Western-centric perspectives dominant? Are some methods / resources overused? Does funding correlate with these patterns?*
  * Example: **30 papers** show overlap between funders / affiliations and the provenance of research materials, but the authors explicitly warn that this **does not establish causality**.
